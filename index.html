English Grammar

English Grammar Complete Summary

English grammar is the system of rules that helps us form correct sentences, communicate clearly, and express ideas accurately. It includes parts of speech, sentence structure, tenses, punctuation, voice, narration, and correct usage of articles, pronouns, modifiers, and conjunctions.

Parts of Speech

Nouns, Verbs, Adjectives

Words are classified based on their function in a sentence.

Tenses

Past, Present, Future

Verb forms show when an action happens.

Sentence

Subject + Predicate

Every sentence should have a subject and a meaningful verb.

Usage

Articles & Modifiers

Correct grammar uses articles, pronouns, and adjectives appropriately.

1. Parts of Speech

Noun

A noun names a person, place, thing, animal, or idea.

  • Rahul
  • Delhi
  • book
  • happiness

Pronoun

A pronoun replaces a noun.

  • he, she, it, they, we, I

Verb

A verb shows action, state, or occurrence.

  • run, write, is, think, dance

Adjective

An adjective describes a noun or pronoun.

  • happy child
  • blue sky
  • tall building

Adverb

An adverb describes a verb, adjective, or another adverb.

  • quickly, very, happily, often

Preposition

A preposition shows relationship between words.

  • in, on, at, under, behind, with

Conjunction

A conjunction joins words, phrases, or clauses.

  • and, but, or, because, although

Interjection

An interjection expresses sudden emotion.

  • Wow! Oh! Alas!

Article

Articles define whether a noun is specific or general.

  • a, an, the

2. Sentence Structure

Simple Sentence

Contains one independent clause.

She reads a book.

Compound Sentence

Contains two or more independent clauses joined by conjunctions.

She reads a book, and he writes an essay.

Complex Sentence

Contains one independent clause and one or more dependent clauses.

When it rains, we stay indoors.

3. Subject and Predicate

Subject

The subject is the person, place, or thing about which something is said.

The dog barked loudly.

Subject: The dog

Predicate

The predicate tells what the subject does or is.

The dog barked loudly.

Predicate: barked loudly

4. Articles

Indefinite Articles

Used for non-specific nouns.

a book, an apple

Definite Article

Used for specific nouns known to the reader.

the book, the sun

Zero Article

No article is used in certain cases like plural general nouns.

Books are useful.

5. Tenses

Tense Use Example
Simple Present Routine / facts She writes everyday.
Present Continuous Action happening now She is writing now.
Present Perfect Action completed recently / experience She has written the letter.
Present Perfect Continuous Action started in the past and continues She has been writing since morning.
Simple Past Finished action in the past She wrote a letter yesterday.
Past Continuous Ongoing action in past She was writing when I arrived.
Past Perfect Action before another past action She had written the letter before he came.
Simple Future Upcoming action She will write a letter.
Future Continuous Action ongoing in future She will be writing at 8 pm.

6. Active and Passive Voice

Active Voice

The subject does the action.

Riya writes a poem.

Passive Voice

The object receives the action.

A poem is written by Riya.

Rule

Passive voice is formed with be + past participle.

Object + be + V3 + by + Subject

7. Direct and Indirect Speech

Direct Speech

Exact words spoken by the speaker.

He said, “I am late.”

Indirect Speech

Reported words without using quotation marks.

He said that he was late.

Key Changes

  • Pronouns change
  • Tense usually shifts back
  • Time words may change

8. Modals

Can / Could

Ability / permission / possibility

I can swim.

May / Might

Possibility

It may rain.

Should / Must

Advice / obligation

You must complete the task.

9. Prepositions and Conjunctions

Prepositions

Show time, place, direction, or relationship.

in the room, on the table, at 9 o’clock

Conjunctions

Join words, phrases, or clauses.

and, but, although, because, if, while

Examples

She came before I left.
I stayed because it was raining.

10. Subject-Verb Agreement

Rule

The verb must agree with the subject in number and person.

He writes.
They write.

Singular Subject

Use singular verb forms.

The boy runs.

Plural Subject

Use plural verb forms.

The boys run.

11. Question Tags

Rule

Question tags repeat the auxiliary verb or do/did/does.

You are late, aren’t you?

Examples

She can swim, can’t she?
They are playing, aren’t they?

Positive / Negative

If the sentence is positive, the tag is usually negative.

He is tall, isn’t he?

12. Punctuation Rules

Full Stop

Used at the end of a sentence.

I am learning English.

Comma

Used to separate items, clauses, or phrases.

We bought apples, oranges, and mangoes.

Question Mark

Used at the end of a question.

Where are you going?

Exclamation Mark

Used to show strong emotion.

Wow! What a beautiful view!

Colon

Used before a list or explanation.

You need three things: pen, notebook, and book.

Semicolon

Used to link closely related independent clauses.

I was tired; I still finished my work.

13. Degrees of Comparison

Positive

This book is good.

Comparative

This book is better than that one.

Superlative

This is the best book in the library.

14. Transformation of Sentences

Affirmative to Negative

She is happy. → She is not unhappy.

Assertive to Interrogative

He is coming. → Is he coming?

Exclamatory to Assertive

What a beautiful flower! → It is a beautiful flower.

15. Common Errors

Subject-Verb Agreement

Incorrect: The students was late.
Correct: The students were late.

Article Mistakes

Incorrect: She is a honest girl.
Correct: She is an honest girl.

Preposition Mistakes

Incorrect: She is good in English.
Correct: She is good at English.

16. Grammar Rules in One Line

A sentence must have a subject and a predicate.
The verb must agree with the subject.
Articles, pronouns, verbs, and prepositions must be used correctly.
Tenses show the time of action.
Punctuation helps the sentence become clear and correct.
